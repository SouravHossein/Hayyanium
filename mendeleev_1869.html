On the Relationship of the Properties
of the Elements to Their Atomic Weights

Presented to the Russian Chemical Society, 6 March 1869

Д. И. Менделеевъ  ·  D. I. Mendeleev

❧

"The elements, if arranged according to their atomic weights, exhibit an evident periodicity of properties. Elements which are similar regarding their chemical properties either have similar atomic weights, or have their atomic weights increasing regularly."

— D. Mendeleev, March 1869  |  Transl. Carmen Giunta, Le Moyne College

How to Read This Table

Mendeleev's 1869 arrangement is the inverse of modern convention:

  • Atomic weight increases downward within each vertical column.
  • Each column is a period of increasing weight.
  • Each horizontal row groups chemically analogous elements.
  • H stands alone above all, its placement uncertain.

This is opposite to today's table, where periods run horizontally and groups run vertically.

Predicted Elements

Gaps marked ?=xx are Mendeleev's deliberate predictions for undiscovered elements:

  • ?=68 — Eka-aluminium
    Discovered as Gallium in 1875.
  • ?=70 — Eka-silicon
    Discovered as Germanium in 1886.
  • ?=45 — Eka-boron
    Discovered as Scandium in 1879.
  • ?=180 — Heavy analogue of Ti/Zr; later resolved as Hafnium (178.5).

Archaic Symbols

  • J — Jod
    Iodine (I in modern use).
  • Ur — Uranium
    Now U; its atomic weight was corrected from 116 to 240 by Mendeleev himself.
  • Di — Didymium
    Later separated into Pr and Nd.
  • Yt — Yttrium
    Now Y; weight corrected.
  • Er — Erbium
    Atomic weight uncertain in 1869.

Errors in the 1869 Table

Mendeleev acknowledged and later corrected several mistakes:

  • Tl (Thallium) & Pb (Lead) placed below Cs and Ba — driven by their +1/+2 states; both were repositioned by 1871.
  • Hg & Au wrongly placed; corrected in subsequent editions.
  • Ur (Uranium) listed at 116; Mendeleev later doubled it to 240 (modern: 238).
  • Te (Tellurium) flagged with "128?" — Mendeleev insisted the weight was wrong; in fact it is 127.6, and the anomaly is explained by isotopes (unknown until the 20th c.).
  • Rare earths (Ce, La, Di, Er, Yt) placed at incorrect atomic weights; corrected by 1870.

What Made It Revolutionary

  • Gaps for undiscovered elements — not mere tidying, but explicit scientific predictions with estimated weights and properties.
  • Property over weight — when weight and chemistry conflicted (Te/I), Mendeleev trusted chemistry and correctly predicted a measurement error.
  • Self-correction built in — unlike Newlands, Mendeleev revised atomic weights where needed, treating the system as falsifiable.
  • Formal law — periodic recurrence stated as a testable, quantitative law, not an analogy.

Historical Reception

Initially met with indifference. The discovery of Gallium (1875) by Lecoq de Boisbaudran — matching Mendeleev's eka-aluminium precisely — transformed scepticism into wide acceptance.

Mendeleev received the Davy Medal in 1882 and the Copley Medal in 1905. Element 101 was named Mendelevium in his honour in 1955.

Ni=Co: In 1869 these elements could not be clearly separated by atomic weight (both ≈59). Mendeleev listed them as a single entry, as Newlands had done before him.

J (Iodine): The symbol J derives from the German Jod. Mendeleev placed it correctly after Te despite Te having a higher atomic weight — a remarkable chemical insight vindicated only by the discovery of isotopes.
